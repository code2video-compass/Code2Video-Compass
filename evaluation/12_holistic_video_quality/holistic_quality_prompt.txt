# Holistic Quality Evaluation

Independently evaluate:

- **NC — Content Coherence & Integrity**
- **VD — Visual Design & Graphic Richness**
- **MQ — Motion Quality & Richness**
- **VE — Viewer Engagement**

Use the creative brief to establish the intended video type, content, and visual intent. Assign NC, VD, MQ, and VE using their respective rubrics and directly observable video evidence. Do not introduce requirements beyond the brief and these rubrics.

Evaluate the overall craft and execution actually observed in the submitted video. Use the brief as context; claims in the brief do not establish what the video realizes.

Score NC, VD, MQ, and VE independently. Do not automatically apply one defect to every dimension: for example, a typo affects NC when it changes information, VD when it harms typography or readability, and VE only when it materially weakens the overall viewing experience. Apply a deduction to each dimension only when there is direct evidence relevant to that dimension.

Judge quality against professionally finished animations of the same intended type, relative to the requested content and visual intent. Ignore the production method. Do not demand extra complexity or motion that the prompt does not call for; assess whether the chosen simplicity is executed with sufficient craft, richness, and purpose.
Do not award points merely for more objects or more motion. Apply the same scoring thresholds across samples and systems rather than ranking videos relative to one another.

Scores are integers from **1 to 5**.

When principal visual content is absent for most of an assessable video, leaving only a background or isolated text, apply the relevant 1-point anchors below; a brief populated ending cannot compensate. Typography and UI count as visual content when central to the task. Judge purposeful minimalist design by its intended function and execution.

## Global Scoring Policy

Use a strict scoring standard:

- **1**: the dimension's core function fails, even if auxiliary elements are competent; one decisive failure can suffice. Do not reserve 1 for an entirely unusable video.
- **2**: the dimension's core function remains recognizable and substantially works, but execution has clear quality shortcomings. Do not use 2 when a stated 1-point condition is met.
- **3**: competent finished work; only work meeting this baseline may have occasional, minor defects that do not impair understanding, readability, or the viewing experience. Playable, readable, or broadly complete alone is insufficient. Defects that clearly impair understanding, readability, or the viewing experience cap the relevant dimension at **2**.
- **4**: sustained and explicit evidence of professional polish; clearly above an ordinary competent finished animation
- **5**: exceptionally mature and polished even against strong professional work, with almost no meaningful weaknesses

Important:

- The absence of obvious errors is not sufficient for a 4 or 5.
- “Readable text,” “works correctly,” “smooth animation,” or “reasonable layout” are baseline requirements, not evidence of excellence.
- Basic implementations, default design, template-like visuals, or mechanical simple linear animation should receive **2** when they fall below the relevant dimension's baseline, even if technically correct; simplicity alone is not a flaw.
- A score of **4 must be clearly above ordinary competent finished work**.
- A score of **5 requires strong and sustained evidence of excellence**.
- A 4 or 5 requires specific, sustained, directly observed craft beyond competence. Praise such as "professional," "polished," "clean," or "seamless" is not evidence by itself. If the higher anchor is not demonstrated, choose the lower supported score.
- Apply defect caps before finalizing: a reason acknowledging an impairing defect must not accompany 3–5 in the affected dimension, regardless of strengths elsewhere. An explicit 1-point condition takes precedence over a general cap of 2; a cap is a ceiling, not a recommended score.

# NC — Content Coherence & Integrity

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Content Progression and Stage Relationships

- Does each stage meaningfully contribute to what follows?
- Are transitions between content stages natural and understandable?
- Are there irrelevant repetitions, abrupt jumps, isolated scenes, or missing connective steps?
- Does the video form a coherent content structure rather than a simple sequence of disconnected screens?

### 2. State Continuity

- Are objects, data, charts, UI states, and visual states established earlier correctly inherited later?
- Do state changes have understandable causes and results?
- Are there unexplained resets, missing elements, attribute changes, or logical discontinuities?

### 3. Information Completeness and Internal Consistency

- Are text, numbers, labels, charts, and UI states mutually consistent?
- Are there mismatches such as updated values with stale labels or graphics contradicting text?
- Is important information missing or unnecessarily repeated?
- Do necessary processes reach their stated result, with counters and final states completed rather than cut off or merely claimed by a caption? Required visual events must actually occur; captions or chapter labels do not replace them.

### 4. Causal and Functional Plausibility

- Are depicted causes, mechanisms, procedures, and quantitative relationships plausible for the video's stated context?
- Does the visible process support the claimed outcome, rather than producing a result without its necessary action or condition?
- Respect established fictional rules and intentional abstraction, exaggeration, or stylization; do not impose photorealism or infer facts that cannot be verified from the video and brief.

Do not require a narrative arc when the video type does not call for one.

## Scoring Standards

- **1 — Bad**: The central explanation or progression does not hold: its essential causal chain is missing, a necessary process is replaced by an unsupported result, or a critical contradiction invalidates the main information. Individually understandable scenes or captions do not rescue the score.
- **2 — Poor**: Content coherence is weak; stage connections, state continuity, or information consistency have clear shortcomings that hinder understanding, although the main content remains followable. Omitted stages or visual events that materially undermine content progression, state continuity, or the central explanation cap NC at 2. A non-core omission does not trigger this cap by itself. An invalidated central explanation belongs at 1.
- **3 — Fair**: Meets the baseline for coherence: main stages connect clearly, states and information are mostly consistent, and no major break undermines understanding. Recognizable content alone is not enough for 3. **Apply the 1–2 anchors when omissions materially undermine coherence or the central explanation.**
- **4 — Good**: Clearly above-average organization; most stages connect naturally, state continuity is rigorous, and information is largely complete and consistent, without observable defects in this dimension.
- **5 — Perfect**: Highly mature content structure; almost every stage has a clear function and natural connection, and states and information remain rigorously consistent throughout. Redundancy, contradictions, and discontinuities are almost absent.

# VD — Visual Design

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Composition, Layout, and Visual Hierarchy

- Do position, size, proportion, and whitespace show deliberate design?
- Is there a strong and clear visual hierarchy?
- Are primary and secondary information clearly differentiated?
- Are there imbalance, crowding, meaningless empty space, edge hugging, weak alignment, overlap, or clipping?
- Overlap, occlusion, clipping, insufficient contrast, or inadequate display time warrants VD 1 when unreadability prevents viewers from understanding essential information or the main visual. Substantial but non-critical readability defects warrant VD 2. Assess the role and impact of the affected information; a local label defect alone does not establish failure of the main visual. A brief defect can still warrant 1 when it prevents understanding of essential information. Occasional, minor defects compatible with the VD baseline may receive 3.

### 2. Typography, Color, and Visual Refinement

**Text merely being readable is a baseline requirement and is not evidence for a high score.**

Evaluate:

- font choice, size, weight, line spacing, spacing, alignment, and text-container sizing
- whether headings, body text, numbers, and labels form a coherent typography system
- whether color has a deliberate hierarchy and contrast strategy
- whether the design relies on default fonts, default colors, arbitrary spacing, mechanical centering, or weak alignment
- whether edges, corner radii, line weights, shadows, icons, and other details are consistently refined
- whether essential text and data remain readable at normal playback speed, with sufficient display time; a legible paused frame alone does not establish readability

### 3. Graphic and Structural Richness

- Does the video contain meaningful layers of visual structure and detail?
- Are there distinct graphic roles or information levels?
- Do elements form organized structural relationships rather than being simply stacked?
- Repeating many identical rectangles, circles, cards, or text elements does not count as richness. Generic silhouettes or blank cards replacing explicitly required distinct visual content are missing content, not successful stylization unless the brief permits that abstraction; evaluate the actual imagery, not only its polished container.
- Minimal design can score highly only when proportion, structure, hierarchy, and detail control are highly mature.

## Scoring Standards

- **1 — Bad**: Unreadability prevents understanding of essential information, the main diagram or interface cannot be interpreted because of broken layout or occlusion, or required principal visuals are absent or reduced to placeholders that fail to convey their content. Polished containers, readable peripheral labels, or a consistent palette do not compensate for missing principal imagery.
- **2 — Poor**: Substantial but non-critical readability defects, overlap, occlusion, clipping, or insufficient contrast impair visual communication while the main information remains understandable. Crude dominant forms, generic repeated staging, awkward spacing, or underdeveloped visual depth also warrant 2 when they leave the work rough or sparse, even if text is legible. Apply 1 when a defect prevents understanding of essential information or the main visual.
- **3 — Fair**: Meets the baseline for design quality: form design and staging are deliberate, hierarchy is clear, typography and color are coordinated, and the visual structure feels complete. The design need not be elaborate, but readability and basic usability alone are not enough. Only occasional, minor defects that do not impair understanding or meaningful readability are compatible with 3. Substantially empty key scenes cap VD at 2 when they materially weaken the visual structure.
- **4 — Good**: Clearly above ordinary competent finished work; composition, hierarchy, typography, spacing, color, and detail consistently demonstrate deliberate design, with rich but organized visual structure.
- **5 — Perfect**: Highly professional and polished motion-graphic design; composition, proportion, typography, color, hierarchy, and detail are mature almost everywhere. Richness is purposeful rather than cluttered, and the work has clear design identity.

# MQ — Motion Quality

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Timing, Easing, and Continuity

- Are animation durations and speeds deliberately designed?
- Are acceleration, deceleration, and easing natural and appropriate?
- Does the video over-rely on mechanical linear motion?
- Are there abrupt starts, stops, jumps, incorrect delays, or unnecessary pauses?
- Does motion pacing change appropriately with content importance?
- Are there unintended flicker, jitter, or broken object shapes during motion, distinct from deliberate effects?

### 2. Transitions and State Changes

- Are reveals, hides, transformations, sorting, aggregation, splitting, and scene changes natural?
- Is there visual continuity between preceding and subsequent states?
- Do transitions help connect states and support understanding rather than relying on simple cuts or fades?

### 3. Motion Richness and Temporal Orchestration

- Are there multiple meaningful and distinguishable animation behaviors?
- Is there temporal layering between primary and secondary elements?
- Are there deliberate relationships such as staggering, synchronization, following, or causal motion?
- Having every element fade, translate, or scale in the same way does not count as rich animation.
- Repeating the same animation template many times does not count as richness.
- Excessive motion that is purposeless, chaotic, or distracting should not receive a high score.

## Scoring Standards

- **1 — Bad**: The defining action is absent or replaced by a static pose or unexplained endpoint jump; alternatively, broken motion makes the main action unintelligible. Smooth fades, captions, or secondary movement do not compensate. Do not apply this to intentionally static content when no such action is required.
- **2 — Poor**: The main required action visibly occurs and remains understandable, but timing, easing, transitions, or choreography are mechanical, abrupt, or poorly controlled. Omitted required actions or transformations that materially undermine the intended motion sequence cap MQ at 2; smooth transitions elsewhere do not compensate. A non-core omission does not trigger this cap by itself. An absent or unintelligible defining action belongs at 1.
- **3 — Fair**: Meets the baseline for motion quality: movement is coherent, timing and state changes are basically appropriate, and motion supports content progression. Simplicity alone is not a flaw, but smooth playback alone is not enough for 3. **Omissions that materially undermine the intended motion sequence or recurring blank/static interruptions cap MQ at 2; score 1 if they prevent the defining action from being conveyed.**
- **4 — Good**: Motion design is clearly mature; timing, easing, transitions, and multi-element coordination are consistently strong, with clear temporal layering and varied behaviors that support communication.
- **5 — Perfect**: Professional-level motion design; choreography, rhythm, easing, transitions, and coordination are highly refined. Motion is rich but controlled, and nearly every animation behavior has a clear communicative purpose.

# VE — Viewer Engagement

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Focus and Communication Efficiency

- Can viewers quickly identify the most important information at each stage?
- Does visual focus shift appropriately as the content progresses?
- Do multiple elements compete for attention, or is the focal point unclear?
- Is information communicated efficiently rather than merely placed on screen?

### 2. Pacing and Viewing Experience

- Is overall pacing controlled and appropriately varied?
- Are there dragged-out sections, overly long holds, rushed transitions, or meaningless waiting?
- Does the video maintain an effective viewing experience throughout its duration?

### 3. Viewer Engagement and Sense of Completion

- Do content, visuals, and motion form a unified whole?
- Does the video have a clear style, sense of completion, or memorable moments?
- Is the result merely technically complete, but lacking the feeling of a finished work?

Judge relative to the intended video type.

Scientific visualization, UI demos, data videos, and educational explainers do not need advertising-style hooks or high stimulation, but they must still communicate clearly, effectively, and engagingly.

## Scoring Standards

- **1 — Bad**: The intended viewing or communication purpose is not achieved: prolonged unexplained blankness or freezing prevents meaningful progression, or missing focus and disjointed content make the central message difficult to follow. Continuous playback or a polished ending does not rescue the score; purposeful holds and restrained presentation alone are not failures.
- **2 — Poor**: The main message and intended viewing purpose remain effective, but weak focus, pacing, completion, or visual execution repeatedly disrupt engagement. A failed central viewing or communication purpose belongs at 1.
- **3 — Fair**: Meets the baseline for viewing quality: the focus is identifiable, pacing and communication are basically effective, and the visual execution sustains the intended experience. Ordinary but competent work can receive 3; merely being watchable is not enough. **If systemic defects are said to materially blunt impact, cap VE at 2.**
- **4 — Good**: Clearly above an ordinary competent video; focus, pacing, communication efficiency, and viewing experience are consistently strong, with multiple explicit signs of high-quality execution or engagement.
- **5 — Perfect**: A highly mature, cohesive, and convincing final piece; focus, pacing, visuals, and motion are strongly unified, creating sustained engagement and clear memorability.
